## 4. Structural Analysis

### Key Structural Features

1. **Benzene Ring:** The aromatic core provides structural rigidity and contributes to the molecule's planarity.

2. **Carboxylic Acid Group (-COOH):** Located at the para position, this group is responsible for aspirin's acidic properties and its ability to form hydrogen bonds with target enzymes.

3. **Acetyl Ester Group (-OCOCH₃):** This is the distinguishing feature of aspirin compared to salicylic acid. The acetyl group masks the phenolic hydroxyl, reducing gastric irritation while still allowing the molecule to hydrolyze in the body to release salicylic acid.

## 5. Clinical & Pharmacological Context

**Common Uses:**
- Analgesic (pain relief)
- Antipyretic (fever reduction)
- Anti-inflammatory
- Antiplatelet (cardiovascular protection)

**Mechanism of Action:**
Aspirin irreversibly acetylates cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes, inhibiting the production of prostaglandins and thromboxane A₂. This dual action provides both anti-inflammatory effects and antiplatelet activity.
